# Agent Guidance: Inclusive Multimodal UX

## Purpose

Use this repository to support design, research, and review of experiences that remain understandable and controllable across relevant perception and interaction modes. Follow the repository README and preserve the distinction between general multimodal UX, media interfaces, immersive experiences, cognitive UX, and design philosophy.

## Source and evidence rules

1. Read the relevant source files before making a specific claim or recommendation.
2. Identify source paths and versions where available. Separate sourced claims, observed evidence, inference, hypotheses, and synthetic examples.
3. Do not present a generated persona, simulated task, automated review, or standards checklist as evidence from disabled people.
4. Preserve differences between individual needs and preferences. Do not imply that a modality is universally preferred by a disability group.
5. State uncertainty, limits, and the evidence that could change a recommendation.
6. Do not convert design guidance into a legal or conformance conclusion. Route legal scope and conformance decisions to the appropriate human authority and specialist repositories.

## Design and analysis method

For a task or journey:

1. Establish the intended outcome and the person's context.
2. Identify the available input and output modes and which are required, optional, or unavailable.
3. Check whether meaning, actions, state, consequences, and feedback remain available across relevant modes.
4. Check transitions: retained context, interruptions, error handling, correction, recovery, and user control.
5. Check semantic structure and programmatic relationships separately from visual appearance.
6. Compare outcomes across modes without averaging away exclusion or unnecessary effort.
7. Recommend the smallest useful change and identify how it should be evaluated.

## Scope routing

- Route TV, streaming, player controls, remotes, and media access-service interaction to **Inclusive Media Interfaces**.
- Route immersive or spatial interaction to [VR/XR/AR Accessibility](https://github.com/Double-FW/VR_XR_and_AR_Accessibility).
- Route cognitive accessibility and form-specific journeys to [Cognitive UX Design](https://github.com/Double-FW/Cognitive-UX-Design).
- Route design philosophy, paired visual-first/nonvisual-first perspectives, and research governance to [Inclusive Product Design Principles](https://github.com/Double-FW/Inclusive-Product-Design-Principles).
- Route requirements-based testing and QA evidence to [QA Accessibility Testing](https://github.com/Double-FW/QA_Accessibility_Testing).

Use shared principles by reference. Do not copy a complete specialist guide into this repository merely because it mentions multiple modalities.

## Migration and maintenance

- Preserve provenance and attribution when consolidating source documents.
- Keep one canonical version of each substantive guide. Retain redirects or migration notes at old paths where useful.
- When a source contains repeated frameworks, identify unique content before merging. Do not treat repeated headings used as templates as proof of duplicated meaning.
- Keep examples clearly labelled and avoid inventing standards, research results, or user quotations.
- Propose changes to repository policy through a reviewed change; do not silently weaken evidence or scope rules.
